# VaakMitra validation, instructor-loop and learning-architecture report — clinician review draft

**Status:** PROPOSED, not approved. **Version:** draft 0.2, 2026-09-14. This document is a review packet, not a consent form, ethics approval, completed validation, or change to `VAAKMITRA_FINAL_SSOT.md`. The coaching-loop, learning-path and federated-learning sections are **design proposals**, not implemented or authorized behavior. If accepted, freeze the protocol and apply the SSOT change-control procedure before data collection, threshold fitting, or product-behavior changes. Applicable IDs: PG-02/08, DEC-008/011/013/015/019, FUS-001..007, GOV-001/002/005/006, DATA-001..009/013, SES-001..005, UX-001..008, RL-001..010, PRV-001..010, GATE-03..06.

## 1. Question and intended claim

Can a **frozen, fully local, word-level** VaakMitra system distinguish a spoken approved Tamil target word from a different/partial/invalid attempt in therapist-supported autistic children aged 7–14, while abstaining when evidence is weak? This is **not** a phoneme score, pronunciation diagnosis, autism assessment, measure of a child's worth/ability, or proof that the app improves therapy outcomes. The primary safety error is a wrong word labelled `PASS`. `UNSCORABLE` is a valid safety outcome, not a hidden failure.

The clinician reviews the exact active-word subset, accepted Tamil/dialect variants, child-facing prompt/response wording, sensory controls, retry/break bounds, and exclusions before recruitment. Only words meeting the reference/classifier/held-out gates can be evaluated for activation; the 100-word registry is not an assertion that all 100 are supported (DATA-010/012). A whole-word practice approach is consistent with [ASHA's description of core-vocabulary treatment](https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/clinical-topics/articulation-and-phonology/), but that source does **not** prove VaakMitra's chosen prompts or model will benefit autistic Tamil-speaking children; this must be evaluated with clinicians and families.

## 2. Ethical entry conditions and child experience

Before **any** child-audio research use, obtain actual guardian/LAR permission for the named protocol and recording scope, applicable institutional ethics determination, and a developmentally appropriate child-assent/ongoing willingness procedure. This follows the [ICMR child-research guidance on permission and assent](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/resource-guidelines/National_Ethical_Guidelines_for_BioMedical_Research_Involving_Children_0.pdf); the exact applicable determination belongs to the institution and responsible ethics body, not this document. An independent authorized administrator verifies the dated evidence; the assistant and therapist cannot impersonate a guardian or ethics committee. Existing audio is not retroactively authorized by a later receipt (GOV-001). The institution's ethics/clinical lead determines whether/how an existing recording may be used; if it cannot, exclude it.

Recruit only children for whom a responsible clinician confirms age, Tamil exposure, relevant speech support, ability to participate without undue burden, and any needed accommodations. Record pseudonymous age band and relevant language/dialect and device context; do not require a particular spoken ability as a condition of dignity or access. The child may refuse, pause, use alternative communication, or stop at any moment without penalty. A caregiver/clinician can stop. Use short familiar activities within the SSOT's 60–900-second session bound, quiet default presentation, visible listening state, no microphone during avatar playback, and no numerical or shaming feedback (SES-002, UX-001..006). The protocol records breaks, distress/withdrawal, and capture failures; it never rewards remaining seated.

Raw research recordings, if authorized, stay in controlled local research storage outside the product database under a clinician/ethics-approved retention and deletion schedule. No child audio, transcript, embeddings, precise scores, or identifiers leave the device through routine product sync (DATA-009, PRV-001..010). Record exact collection-device/build and capture-pipeline versions.

## 3. Data collection: controlled plus naturalistic

Collect complete **attempts** with the approved target word and prompt ID, not just clipped syllables. A therapist offers age-appropriate, randomized approved target activities; do not demand a child deliberately say a wrong word or repeatedly fail. Obtain negative examples from spontaneous other-word/confusable/partial responses and, only if the clinician finds it acceptable, a neutral low-pressure alternative-word activity. Preserve no-speech, whisper/soft voice, pauses, repeated starts, adult background speech, prompt leakage, noise/clipping, and cancellation as labelled challenge cases. Capture on representative daily-use Android microphones in quiet and ordinary home/clinic noise; do not mix audio from avatar playback into a supposedly child-only attempt. Cap attempts and breaks per approved plan. Keep one family/household and all repeated sessions, duplicates and augmentations in one data split (DATA-003/007).

The **current 11 child recordings from one pseudonymous child** are metadata-only, with no verified consent/ethics basis, canonical target, age, or two item-level ratings. They are not authorized validation data today. Even if individually authorized later, they can be a feasibility/annotation pilot or belong to only **one** split; they cannot establish child-disjoint calibration and final-test performance. Adult/therapist clips are engineering inputs, not substitutes for target-child validity (DATA-001/002).

## 4. Independent ground truth

Two credential-verified, Tamil-competent speech-language clinicians/qualified raters independently hear each authorized full attempt **without seeing the model output or each other's label**. They record: pseudonymous child/household/session/sample IDs; audio SHA-256; age band and capture context; assigned canonical word and accepted variant; label `TARGET`, `OTHER` (actual word if confidently known), `PARTIAL_OR_NO_MEANINGFUL_WORD`, `CAPTURE_INVALID`, or `UNCERTAIN`; reason code; reviewer ID, time and evidence provenance. They distinguish word identity from articulation quality or disability-related speech variation. A third qualified adjudicator resolves disagreements while preserving original ratings. Report raw agreement and Cohen's kappa (plus class-wise disagreement); do not turn `UNCERTAIN` into target by default (DATA-005/006, GOV-002/006).

The clinical lead defines whether a repeated beginning, dialect variant, delayed response or intelligible approximation counts as `TARGET` **before** annotators start. This is why the protocol requests Tamil/dialect-competent clinicians and individualized interpretation: [ASHA's autism guidance](https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/clinical-topics/autism/) and [multilingual-service guidance](https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/professional-issues/multilingual-service-delivery/) stress culturally and linguistically responsive assessment. If neither rater can determine the word reliably, the reference label remains `UNCERTAIN`; it is excluded from the target-vs-wrong primary denominator and reported separately. Capture-invalid cases test abstention, not word correctness.

## 5. Frozen splits and analysis

Before model tuning, pre-register a child/household-disjoint split: a pilot/development group, a calibration group used only to fit the word-match calibrator and thresholds, and an **untouched final-test group**. Stratify prospectively by ages 7–10 and 11–14, language/dialect where feasible, site and microphone/device; keep every recording from a child and derived/near-duplicate relatives in the same group. No final-test child contributes to teacher/student training, classifier, normalization tuning, reference selection, prompt selection, threshold fitting, or canary construction (DATA-003/004/008/013). This implements the SSOT's stronger child-level rule and is aligned with the [FDA/Health Canada/MHRA GMLP principles of representative participants and independent train/test data](https://www.fda.gov/media/153486/download); the FDA document is used as design guidance, not a claim of FDA jurisdiction or approval.

**Planning target for feasibility, not a powered guarantee:** recruit a small, separate pilot/development group, then approximately 60 additional independent children, provisionally 30 calibration and 30 untouched final-test children, balanced across the two age bands. Pilot children cannot enter the untouched final test. Aim for at least 600 adjudicated non-target word attempts and 300 target attempts in the final test, spread across children, words, devices and conditions over short sessions. A statistician and clinician must approve/revise the sample-size calculation using expected false-PASS rate, within-child correlation, word coverage, and subgroup precision **before** enrollment. Never stop early because interim results look favorable. If enrollment or per-word coverage is inadequate, report the uncertainty and keep those words/slices inactive; never fill deficits with duplicated or converted speech.

Fit logistic/isotonic calibration and choose retry/pass/abstain thresholds only on the calibration children using the exact live feature pipeline. Freeze and cross-hash model, student if applicable, classifier, adult reference bank, calibration, VAD, normalization, vocabulary, dataset manifest, app build and code commit. Run the frozen system once on the final-test children. Any tuning after seeing final-test results makes that set development data and requires a new independent final test (DEC-015, DATA-004/008/013).

## 6. Predeclared outcomes and decision rule

**Primary denominator:** all adjudicated, capture-valid `OTHER` word attempts whose assigned target is an active evaluated word. **Primary numerator:** those attempts the frozen system marks `PASS`. Report wrong-word false-PASS point estimate and a two-sided 95% confidence interval; account for within-child clustering (child-level bootstrap or a pre-specified equivalent), with a binomial exact interval as a transparent secondary analysis. The SSOT ceiling is **2.0% overall**. This draft proposes the stricter release rule that **the 95% upper bound must also be ≤2.0%**; that additional rule needs clinician/statistician approval and a matching SSOT change before it is binding. With zero errors, even 183 *independent* wrong attempts are needed for a two-sided exact upper bound near 2%, by the [NIST exact-binomial method](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/software/dataplot/refman2/auxillar/exacbici.htm); repeated attempts from one child are **not** independent and require clustering-aware analysis ([TRIPOD-Cluster reporting guidance](https://www.bmj.com/content/380/bmj-2022-071058)). A count of attempts alone is not proof of power.

Report separately: false PASS on `PARTIAL_OR_NO_MEANINGFUL_WORD`, PASS/RETRY precision and recall, `UNSCORABLE` frequency and reasons, risk–coverage curve, calibration error/Brier score, confusion by word, latency, and device/offline failure. Predeclare age-band, word, device, quiet/noisy, pause/repetition and other ethically appropriate subgroup views. The existing SSOT blocks/disables any tested word or predeclared subgroup with false PASS >5.0%; sparse groups with imprecise estimates remain **not validated**, never silently pooled into an overall PASS. An invalid capture, prompt leakage, model timeout, or artifact mismatch must abstain; a disagreement cannot be upgraded to PASS without calibrated evidence (FUS-001..007).

For the clinician-facing experience evaluation, observe whether the child can stop, whether prompt/listen turns are clear, signs of distress or overload, breaks, caregiver comprehension of `UNSCORABLE`, and any misleading praise. These observations are not a speech-accuracy metric. A clinician reviews adverse events and protocol deviations before any child-facing expansion. The final report must state exclusions, missingness, disagreement, split hashes, operating point, uncertainty, and limitations, following the [STARD-AI reporting guideline](https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/the-stard-ai-reporting-guideline-for-diagnostic-accuracy-studies-using-artificial-intelligence/) where applicable. STARD-AI improves reporting transparency; it does not supply a VaakMitra-specific sample size or validate this device.

### One validation **recording visit**, step by step

1. A study coordinator checks the approved protocol version, actual guardian permission, applicable ethics determination, child eligibility and scope **before** opening research capture. If any condition is absent, do not record for research. Confirm the child's current willingness using communication appropriate to that child; absence of resistance is not presumed assent.
2. The clinician selects sensory settings and a short approved word set. The child sees a predictable start/stop explanation; caregiver or clinician remains able to end the visit. A refusal means stop, with no pressure or penalty.
3. The operator verifies the local microphone, no unintended network transfer, clock, device/build ID, approved prompt asset hashes, and recording destination. A sample-quality check uses adult/operator audio, not unconsented child speech.
4. The avatar or clinician gives one approved Tamil word prompt while the child microphone is **off**. When prompt playback ends, a clear listening indicator appears and capture begins. The child may pause/stop at any time. Naturalistic variants and quiet/ordinary-noise conditions are scheduled without overstimulation.
5. Each attempt is captured once with target/prompt ID and capture context. The operator may offer a clinician-approved neutral cue or break, but **no unvalidated model PASS/RETRY feedback** is shown in this observational phase. Do not replay, transcribe in the browser, or convert the child's voice to an adult voice as ground truth.
6. After a small bounded set of attempts, ask whether the child wants a break or to finish. Stop for distress, refusal, fatigue, technical failure, or caregiver/clinician request. Record reason codes and deviations without pathologizing the child.
7. At visit end, close the microphone, confirm raw files are in authorized encrypted research storage, generate checksums, and record access/retention metadata. No child audio is put into product logs, repository, training set, or outbound sync by default. Honor withdrawal according to the approved terms.
8. Only then route eligible recordings to two independent blinded qualified raters. Adjudicate disagreements and lock labels. Allocation by child/household is checked before calibration or final-test inference. Pilot results may change the protocol; final-test results may **not** change the frozen threshold without invalidating that test.

This recording visit is **not** the application's 14-day therapist checkup. The checkup is an ordinary clinical contact booked by the caregiver and completed by the assigned therapist; scheduling or a study recording does not imply it happened (CHK-001..004).

## 11. What the current implementation actually does

The guarded browser word route is `frontend/src/pages/TherapyPage.jsx` → `features/wordSession/sessionFlow.js` → `sessionApi.js`. It fetches the signed-in child's assigned plans and `/health/scoring`; when scoring is unavailable it displays a blocked, ungraded state. For an available plan it starts `/api/v1/word-sessions`, reads the server-owned current word, then the child **taps** Start recording, taps Stop recording, and taps Check word. `Slide3_Evaluation.jsx` disables browser speech recognition/repetition on this route, converts the capture to WAV and submits one attempt. The API validates/bounds the WAV, atomically claims the attempt, evaluates quality/VAD, original and normalized model views, classifier/reference evidence and calibration, applies deterministic policy, persists a bounded summary and returns a decision. The frontend refetches the session snapshot before showing the outcome or next word. Pause/Stop cancels in-flight work; the browser has no authority to resume a paused session (SES-001..005, UX-007).

**Implementation gap:** this browser route has a static Pippin companion, not the approved Tamil voiced Unity turn-taking loop. It is manually push-to-record, not automatic back-and-forth; therapist-authored cue selection, evidence-based support classification, and a personalized path are not yet implemented in this route. Its WAV is posted to a local backend; that is **not** the final no-audio-transfer, fully on-device Android architecture. The local FastAPI diagnostic is an adult/synthetic **engineering** harness, not the consumer runtime. Production scoring is presently unavailable because approved child-separated calibration and active artifacts are missing; the correct current result is `UNSCORABLE`, not a clinical PASS. The 11 child recordings were not read for this report. Physical Android/Unity session, mobile student latency/privacy and clinician-content evidence remain separate gates (DEC-013/018, FUS-007, GATE-03..06).

Visual letter teaching is separate from the canonical scored word session and remains silent/unscored when scoring is unavailable. The 14-day checkup reminder is a separate caregiver/therapist workflow; due/overdue never fabricates a completed visit or a correctness score (UX-007, CHK-001..004).

## 13. Proposed instructor loop: teach, listen, identify support need, retry the **whole word**

**Design decision for clinician review, not current implementation:** the avatar is a continuous, predictable instructor who responds to the *attempt and recent support history*, not a static score display. The loop is bounded by the assigned plan's retry/capture/time limits and the child's ability to stop. The term “missing thing” is restricted to a **validated support need at word level**. A word-identity model cannot establish that a specific consonant, vowel, syllable, or articulator movement was omitted. ASHA describes whole-word core-vocabulary work and clinician-chosen treatment strategies, but it does not validate automated sound-level diagnosis from VaakMitra's current evidence ([ASHA speech-sound guidance](https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/clinical-topics/articulation-and-phonology/)). A specific sound/segment cue may be shown only when a qualified therapist has prescribed it for this child/word, or after a separately labelled and validated capability is approved through an SSOT change; it must not be invented from a low-confidence ASR transcript.

### One bounded teaching cycle

1. `PREPARE`: read the child's current therapist plan, approved target word, functional meaning/picture, permitted cues, sensory settings, prior bounded *verified* history and remaining time/captures/retries. There is no diagnosis-severity, emotion, attention or “time seated” inference.
2. `MODEL`: play one approved Tamil **complete-word** example with the microphone off; show a calm matching picture/gesture. The avatar's words and movements come from a reviewed asset set, not an unrestricted generative conversation.
3. `LISTEN`: when playback ends, display the listening indicator and capture one child attempt. Stop, pause, break and refusal are honored immediately. A silent or invalid recording is not interpreted as failure to learn.
4. `CHECK`: run local capture validation and the frozen calibrated word scorer. Evidence produces only `PASS`, `RETRY` or `UNSCORABLE` plus permitted coarse reason codes. No child-facing numeric confidence, guessed transcript, or unapproved sound diagnosis.
5. `CHOOSE ONE SUPPORT`: if `PASS`, acknowledge briefly and advance or revisit later; if capture-invalid/uncertain, ask neutrally whether to try the **whole word** again or pause; if a confident word-level mismatch is validated, select **one** approved support from picture/meaning cue, slower complete-word model, whole-word contrast with a clinician-approved confusable, or therapist-authored child-specific cue. Do not say “you missed /k/” based on word-level evidence.
6. `WHOLE-WORD RETRY`: after the cue and microphone-off playback, return to listening for a **new entire-word attempt**. Do not score a cue fragment as an independent word PASS. The instructor observes whether the chosen cue helped by comparing *separate calibrated full-word attempts*; `UNSCORABLE` is not counted as improvement or failure.
7. `BOUND THE LOOP`: repeat only while the child voluntarily continues and plan limits permit. When retries are exhausted, offer a break or a different therapist-approved word; revisit the difficult word in a later planned session. No policy can keep the child seated, reset the session clock, suppress a break, or override Stop (SES-002..004, UX-001..006).

| Available **validated** evidence | Instructor interpretation | Allowed next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clear target PASS | This attempt matched the approved target under the frozen word-level operating point. | Gentle celebration; next approved word or calm end. One PASS alone does not prove mastery. |
| Clear other/confusable RETRY | The full-word attempt did not match the target; any specific confusable hypothesis needs separately validated support. | One approved whole-word cue, then a new complete-word attempt. |
| Partial/uncertain or unsupported variant | The system cannot safely locate or name a missing segment. | `UNSCORABLE` or a separately validated coarse category; neutral full-word example/recapture, or adult help. No invented sound-level feedback. |
| No speech/noise/clipping/prompt leakage | Capture quality, not the child's ability, is the issue. | Fix capture, offer break, then recapture if the child wants. |
| Model/calibration/version failure or timeout | There is no trusted word judgement. | Pause scoring and request caregiver help; never celebrate a fabricated PASS. |

**Example trace (illustrative, not a claim about a real child):** therapist target `அம்மா` → approved full-word model → child attempt is judged a high-confidence *other word* → avatar says an approved neutral line such as “Let's listen once more,” shows the approved meaning picture, plays the full target with the microphone off → child chooses to try the **entire word** again → new capture is independently assessed. If still RETRY and the plan limit is reached, the avatar offers a break or a different assigned word; it does not keep looping indefinitely or infer a “missing sound.”

The observational **validation recording visit** in Section 9 remains blinded: it collects speech and neutral clinician-led practice but does not expose an unvalidated automated decision or experimental cue policy to the child. This live coaching loop belongs in a **later supervised interactive study** after the word-decision gate and exact prompt assets pass review. The study must separately measure whether cues help, confuse, overload, or increase false reassurance; word-recognition accuracy alone cannot establish instructional benefit.

## 14. Proposed personalized learning path and where reinforcement learning fits

“Understanding the child's condition” here means respecting **therapist-recorded goals, accommodations, language/dialect and the child's current observable practice context**, not automatically diagnosing autism severity, emotion, distress, attention, or developmental level. The therapist owns difficulty tiers and the allowed word/cue graph; the app may propose paths inside those bounds. ASHA's autism guidance emphasizes individual and family context, and its speech-sound guidance describes clinician-selected targets and strategies ([ASHA autism](https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/clinical-topics/autism/), [ASHA speech sounds](https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/clinical-topics/articulation-and-phonology/)). These sources motivate clinician control; the exact VaakMitra progression rules are **project design choices requiring clinical evaluation**, not published clinical constants.

The on-device, pseudonymous learning state uses only consented/allowed fields: target-word ID, therapist-assigned difficulty/support tags, completed `PASS`/`RETRY`/`UNSCORABLE` counts across sessions, which approved cue was tried, whether a **later full-word attempt** was independently judged better, recapture/quality reasons, breaks/stop requests, and versioned dates. No raw audio, embeddings, transcript, voice conversion output, inferred diagnosis severity/emotion, or free-text child profile enters the policy (RL-001..005, PRV-001..010). An `UNSCORABLE` attempt never updates mastery as if it were PASS or RETRY.

**V1 deterministic path:** `introduce functional word → supported practice → independent practice → maintenance/revisit`, with clinician-configured evidence and spacing rules. Repeated reliable success on **different days/conditions**, not one lucky PASS, can propose a slightly harder approved word; repeated clear RETRY triggers one more supportive representation or a scheduled easier related word; repeated capture invalidity triggers technical help, not easier curriculum. The child may pause or stop at every point. The therapist reviews the proposed path at the actual 14-day checkup and can edit/reject it. Difficulty is a teaching choice, not a label of the child (DEC-011/019, CHK-001..004).

**RL decision:** no reinforcement learner controls V1. After enough *consented longitudinal, child-separated* evidence and a separate protocol, a **contextual bandit** may be evaluated offline, then in non-acting shadow mode, and only later considered for carefully supervised active use through SSOT change control. Its action space is limited to clinician-approved **cue or next-word choices**. The deterministic layer still owns correctness, uncertain-result abstention, Stop, breaks, session length, and allowed difficulty. Rewards may reflect verified improvement after support and voluntary return; **time seated is zero reward** (DEC-011, RL-001..010). The 60-child word-validation study validates the scorer, not the benefit or safety of an RL policy. Claiming an optimized learning path needs a separate prospective comparison with clinician-defined outcomes.

## 15. Cloud teacher, local student, and federated learning: decision and threat model

**Recommended V1 topology:** the large AI4Bharat teacher runs only in controlled development/training infrastructure, potentially a private cloud for appropriately licensed adult/public data; it is **not called during a child session**. A compact, versioned student and calibration run on the ordinary Android device. If the absolute product promise is “no child audio files reach servers,” then cloud teacher training cannot use child-session audio; consented child research recordings may be evaluated only in an approved controlled local research environment. Teacher outputs on adult/public or separately authorized research data support distillation, but adult-only success never substitutes for child-separated validation. Signed and hash-verified student/cue bundles are installed **before** sessions, with rollback on incompatibility. The final device works offline (DEC-013/015, DATA-001..009, PRV-001..010). “Cloud teacher” is an optional development location, **not** a cloud speech endpoint.

**Federated learning (FL) is not needed for V1**: local rule-based personalization and a centrally built frozen student require no child device gradients at all. [FedAvg's original paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v54/mcmahan17a.html) defines FL as aggregating device-computed model updates rather than centralizing raw examples; it does **not** make those updates private or trustworthy by itself. [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/blogs/cybersecurity-insights/privacy-attacks-federated-learning) and the original [gradient-leakage paper](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2019/hash/60a6c4002cc7b29142def8871531281a-Abstract.html) show that updates can reveal training information. A small, heterogeneous 60-child study is also a poor basis for claiming user-level privacy or robust federated training; the study's purpose is validation, not fleet training. This last sentence is a project inference from the study design and FL threat model, not a published sample-size rule.

| Threat if FL is added later | Why “weighted gradients” or FL alone do not solve it | Required future control and residual limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gradient inversion / membership inference | One device's update, or a small aggregate, may expose sensitive speech properties even though no WAV is sent ([NIST](https://www.nist.gov/blogs/cybersecurity-insights/privacy-attacks-federated-learning)). | Never send raw/per-attempt gradients. If later justified: large opt-in cohorts, client-level contribution clipping, a formally accounted **user-level differential-privacy** budget, and secure aggregation. Privacy–accuracy trade-offs and residual aggregate/model leakage must be tested ([private FL research](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=BJ0hF1Z0b), [secure aggregation](https://eprint.iacr.org/2017/281.pdf)). |
| Malicious/compromised clients poison or backdoor model | Weighted averaging can let a high-volume client dominate; secure aggregation hides individual updates from inspection and does not prove they are benign. [Model-poisoning research](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/34733) documents this risk. | Cap each device's influence; authenticate enrollment; use anti-replay, update-size/range checks, anomaly/robust-aggregation research compatible with the chosen privacy design, held-out child canaries, independent frozen evaluation and human release approval. There is **no** guarantee all poisoning is detectable. |
| Server distributes altered weights/cues | Gradient protections do not secure outbound updates or a compromised build pipeline. | Sign model and content manifests, verify exact hashes/versions on device, require monotonic/rollback-safe updates, independently test the exact bundle before rollout. Android app signing supports update integrity, but not immunity on compromised devices ([Android app-signing guidance](https://developer.android.com/studio/publish/preparing)). |
| Privacy–robustness conflict | Strong secure aggregation conceals individual updates that some poisoning detectors need; DP noise can reduce sensitivity to rare child speech patterns. | Predeclare a combined threat model and evaluate privacy, poisoning and subgroup performance together; do not claim “secure aggregation + robust filtering” works without a concrete compatible protocol and attack tests ([NIST secure-aggregation explanation](https://www.nist.gov/blogs/cybersecurity-insights/protecting-model-updates-privacy-preserving-federated-learning)). |

If FL is ever proposed, it is a **separate opt-in research project** requiring an amended consent/ethics and SSOT decision, many genuinely independent participating devices, a documented privacy accountant, robust/secure aggregation design, red-team gradient-inversion and poisoning tests, child-disjoint holdout evaluation after *every* candidate update, model signing/rollback, and a decision that the utility gain justifies the new risk. No system can promise “no hacking” absolutely. Until that evidence exists: **no FL client, no child gradients or model updates outbound, no online model learning during a child session**.

## 17. Change-control and verification work if the proposal is accepted

This draft does **not** silently amend the normative SSOT. Before implementing its new behavior, record a versioned SSOT decision with affected IDs and migration/tests. The minimum change-control package is:

1. Define an approved `support_need` taxonomy and exact evidence threshold for each category; preserve `UNSCORABLE` for partial/ambiguous/invalid cases. A missing **sound** remains unsupported unless a separate qualified-label/model-validation track is approved (DEC-003/008/015, FUS-001..007, DATA-002..006).
2. Define the therapist-authored cue registry: Tamil text/audio/picture/gesture assets, allowed child/word/condition, content hash, version, review provenance and fallback. No unreviewed generation or raw model transcript in child-facing copy (GOV-005, UX-002/004/006).
3. Specify the bounded session-loop transition contract and race handling: `MODEL → LISTEN → CHECK → SUPPORT → WHOLE_WORD_RETRY`; one capture/claim at a time; Stop wins; max retries, total captures, elapsed time and required breaks stay authoritative (SES-001..005).
4. Specify therapist-owned difficulty graph, per-word evidence rules, path suggestion/rejection UI and a migration for minimal local pseudonymous state. No condition-severity/emotion inference and no mastery updates from `UNSCORABLE` (DEC-011/019, RL-001..005, PRV-001..010).
5. Test with synthetic decision fixtures first, then consented child-separated scorer evidence and a **separate** supervised cue-usability study. Verify exact Android/Unity prompt-listen exclusion, offline operation, privacy packet/filesystem traces, signed-bundle rollback and adverse-event handling (GATE-01..06).
6. Keep FL out of V1. Any later FL request must start a new privacy/security/clinical protocol, not a hidden model-update feature flag (DEC-011/013, GOV-001, PRV-001..010).
